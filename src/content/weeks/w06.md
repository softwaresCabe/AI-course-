## 6.1 Pretraining: compressing the internet into weights

A **base model** is produced by **pretraining**: next-token prediction over trillions of tokens of web text, books, code, scientific papers and (increasingly) synthetic and multimodal data. To predict text well, the model must implicitly learn grammar, facts, reasoning patterns and world models — next-token prediction is a deceptively rich objective.

A base model is a **document completer**, not an assistant. Ask it a question and it may continue with more questions. Turning it into a helpful assistant is **post-training** (Week 7).

## 6.2 Data is the product

Frontier labs spend enormous effort on data. A typical pipeline:
1. **Collection**: web crawls (e.g. Common Crawl), code repositories, books, papers, licensed data.
2. **Extraction**: HTML → clean text; handle boilerplate, tables, math.
3. **Filtering**: language ID, quality classifiers (e.g. trained to recognize "educational" text), heuristics (length, symbol ratios), toxicity and PII removal.
4. **Deduplication**: exact and near-duplicate removal (MinHash/LSH). Duplicates waste compute and increase memorization.
5. **Decontamination**: remove benchmark test sets so evaluations stay honest.
6. **Mixing**: choose proportions of web/code/math/multilingual data; often **annealing** on the highest-quality data at the end of training.

Open datasets like FineWeb (Hugging Face) document these steps and show how much quality filtering matters: a well-filtered dataset can match a much larger unfiltered one.

## 6.3 Compute: the 6ND rule

Training FLOPs for a dense transformer are approximately:

```
C ≈ 6 · N · D
```

where N = parameters and D = training tokens. Why 6? The forward pass costs ~2 FLOPs per parameter per token (one multiply-add per weight); the backward pass costs ~2× the forward (gradients w.r.t. both activations and weights) → 2 + 4 = 6.

Example: a 7B model on 2T tokens → `6 · 7e9 · 2e12 = 8.4e22 FLOPs`. An accelerator delivering ~1e15 FLOP/s (bf16 dense, order-of-magnitude for a modern datacenter GPU) at **40% Model FLOPs Utilization (MFU)** gives 4e14 effective FLOP/s → 2.1e8 GPU-seconds ≈ **58,000 GPU-hours**. This back-of-envelope skill is gold in interviews and planning.

Inference costs ≈ **2N FLOPs per generated token** (forward only), plus attention over the context.

## 6.4 Scaling laws

**Kaplan et al. (2020)** found loss falls as a smooth **power law** in parameters, data and compute over many orders of magnitude. **Hoffmann et al. (2022, "Chinchilla")** refined the compute-optimal allocation: for a fixed compute budget, scale parameters and tokens roughly **equally**, at about **~20 tokens per parameter**. Chinchilla (70B params, 1.4T tokens) outperformed the much larger Gopher (280B) at the same compute.

In practice, models are now trained **far past** Chinchilla-optimal (hundreds or thousands of tokens per parameter) because **inference cost** depends on N: a smaller model trained longer is cheaper to serve forever. "Compute-optimal for training" ≠ "optimal for deployment".

Other scaling observations:
- **Emergent abilities**: some capabilities appear to jump at scale, though part of the effect depends on how metrics are chosen (smooth log-likelihood vs pass/fail accuracy).
- **Test-time compute scaling**: reasoning models improve by spending more compute at inference (longer chains of thought, search) — a second scaling axis (Week 7).
- **Data constraints**: high-quality human text is finite, motivating synthetic data, multimodal data and repeated epochs.

## 6.5 Mixed precision and numerics at scale

- Train in **bf16** matmuls with **fp32** master weights and optimizer states; some setups use **fp8** matmuls for further speedups.
- **Loss spikes** are common at scale; mitigations include gradient clipping, lower learning rates, QK-norm, z-loss on logits, skipping bad batches and restarting from checkpoints.
- **Checkpoint frequently**; at thousands of GPUs, hardware failures are routine.

## 6.6 Distributed training

A model too large or a dataset too big for one GPU requires parallelism:

- **Data parallelism (DP/DDP)**: replicate the model on every GPU, give each a different slice of the batch, **all-reduce** gradients. Simple; limited by per-GPU memory.
- **ZeRO / FSDP (sharded data parallel)**: shard optimizer states, gradients and even parameters across GPUs, gathering them just-in-time. Dramatically reduces per-GPU memory.
- **Tensor parallelism (TP)**: split individual matmuls across GPUs (e.g. Megatron-LM column/row splits). Needs very fast interconnect (NVLink) — usually within a node.
- **Pipeline parallelism (PP)**: different layers on different GPUs; micro-batches flow through like an assembly line; must manage "bubbles".
- **Sequence/context parallelism**: split the sequence dimension for long contexts.
- **Expert parallelism**: place different MoE experts on different GPUs.

Large runs combine these ("3D/4D parallelism"). The governing constraint is usually **communication bandwidth**, not raw FLOPs — which is why GPU cluster networking (InfiniBand, NVLink) matters as much as the chips.
